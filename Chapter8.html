Chapter 8: The Great Reversal

"The Fundamental Theorem — Differentiation and integration are opposites."

Problem 1 of 12 ⬜

Question: What does the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus say?

(Hint: It's about the relationship between derivatives and integrals.)

Think about addition and subtraction. They undo each other. The derivative and integral do the same thing.

The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus says that differentiation and integration are inverse operations — they undo each other.

  • If you take the derivative of a function and then integrate, you get back the original function (plus a constant).
  • If you integrate a function and then take the derivative, you get back the original function.

Answer: Differentiation and integration are inverse operations — they undo each other.

Problem 2 of 12 ⬜

Question: What is the antiderivative of $2x$?

(Hint: Reverse the Power Rule. What function has derivative $2x$?)

The derivative of $x^2$ is $2x$. So the antiderivative of $2x$ is $x^2$. Don't forget the constant $C$.

$\int 2x \, dx = x^2 + C$

Check: The derivative of $x^2 + C$ is $2x$. ✓

Answer: $x^2 + C$

Problem 3 of 12 ⬜

Question: What is the antiderivative of $3x^2$?

The derivative of $x^3$ is $3x^2$. So the antiderivative of $3x^2$ is $x^3 + C$.

$\int 3x^2 \, dx = x^3 + C$

Check: The derivative of $x^3 + C$ is $3x^2$. ✓

Answer: $x^3 + C$

Problem 4 of 12 ⬜

Question: What is the antiderivative of $x^3$?

Reverse the Power Rule: add 1 to the exponent, then divide by the new exponent.

$x^3 \to x^{3+1} / (3+1) = x^4 / 4$

$\int x^3 \, dx = \frac{x^4}{4} + C$

Check: The derivative of $\frac{x^4}{4} + C$ is $x^3$. ✓

Answer: $\frac{x^4}{4} + C$

Problem 5 of 12 ⬜

Question: Find the area under $y = x^2$ from $x = 0$ to $x = 4$ using the Fundamental Theorem.

(Hint: Find the antiderivative $F(x)$, then compute $F(4) - F(0)$.)

Antiderivative of $x^2$ is $x^3/3$.

$F(4) = 4^3/3 = 64/3$, $F(0) = 0$.

Area = $F(4) - F(0)$.

$F(x) = \int x^2 \, dx = \frac{x^3}{3} + C$

$F(4) = \frac{4^3}{3} = \frac{64}{3}$

$F(0) = \frac{0^3}{3} = 0$

Area = $\frac{64}{3} - 0 = \frac{64}{3}$

Answer: $\frac{64}{3}$

Problem 6 of 12 ⬜

Question: What is the constant of integration and why do we add it?

(Hint: What happens to constants when you take the derivative?)

The derivative of $x^2 + 5$ is $2x$. The derivative of $x^2 - 3$ is also $2x$. So when we integrate $2x$, we don't know which constant was there.

The constant of integration is $C$.

We add it because the derivative of any constant is zero.

So many different functions have the same derivative. When we integrate, we add $C$ to cover all possibilities.

Answer: The constant of integration C is added because the derivative of any constant is zero.

Problem 7 of 12 ⬜

Question: Find the area under $y = 2x$ from $x = 0$ to $x = 5$.

(Hint: Antiderivative of $2x$ is $x^2$.)

$F(x) = x^2$. $F(5) = 25$, $F(0) = 0$. Area = $25 - 0$.

$F(x) = \int 2x \, dx = x^2 + C$

$F(5) = 25$

$F(0) = 0$

Area = $25 - 0 = 25$

Answer: 25

Problem 8 of 12 ⬜

Question: Find the area under $y = x^2 + 1$ from $x = 0$ to $x = 3$.

Antiderivative of $x^2 + 1$ is $\frac{x^3}{3} + x$.

$F(3) = 27/3 + 3 = 9 + 3 = 12$.

$F(0) = 0$.

$F(x) = \int (x^2 + 1) \, dx = \frac{x^3}{3} + x + C$

$F(3) = \frac{27}{3} + 3 = 9 + 3 = 12$

$F(0) = 0 + 0 = 0$

Area = $12 - 0 = 12$

Answer: 12

Problem 9 of 12 ⬜

Question: If you start with $f(x) = x^3$, take its derivative, and then integrate, what do you get?

(Hint: This is the "round trip" — function → derivative → integral.)

Derivative of $x^3$ is $3x^2$. Integral of $3x^2$ is $x^3 + C$.

Start: $f(x) = x^3$

Derivative: $f'(x) = 3x^2$

Integral: $\int 3x^2 \, dx = x^3 + C$

Result: $x^3 + C$

This is the "round trip" — you get back where you started!

Problem 10 of 12 ⬜

Question: Use the Fundamental Theorem to find $\int_1^4 3x^2 \, dx$.

(Hint: Antiderivative of $3x^2$ is $x^3$. Compute $F(4) - F(1)$.)

$F(x) = x^3$

$F(4) = 64$, $F(1) = 1$

Area = $64 - 1$

$F(x) = \int 3x^2 \, dx = x^3 + C$

$F(4) = 4^3 = 64$

$F(1) = 1^3 = 1$

Area = $64 - 1 = 63$

Answer: 63

$f(x) = x^2 \xrightarrow{\text{Derivative}} \text{_____} \xrightarrow{\text{Integral}} \text{_____}$

Enter the two missing expressions.

Derivative of $x^2$ is $2x$. Integral of $2x$ is $x^2 + C$.

$f(x) = x^2 \xrightarrow{\text{Derivative}} 2x \xrightarrow{\text{Integral}} x^2 + C$

Answer: $2x$, $x^2 + C$

Problem 12 of 12 ⬜

Question: If the derivative and integral are opposites, why is the Fundamental Theorem called the "Great Reversal"?

(Hint: It reversed how people thought about slope and area.)

Before the Fundamental Theorem, slope (derivative) and area (integral) were seen as separate. The theorem reversed that thinking.

The Fundamental Theorem is called the "Great Reversal" because it reversed how people thought about calculus.

Before the theorem, slope (derivative) and area (integral) were seen as separate ideas.

The theorem showed that they are opposites — they undo each other.

Answer: It is called the Great Reversal because it showed that slope and area are opposites — they undo each other.
