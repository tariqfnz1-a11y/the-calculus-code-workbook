Chapter 9: The Super-Function — e^x

"e^x is the function that equals its own derivative — the universal growth pattern."

Problem 1 of 10 ⬜

Question: What is the number e approximately equal to?

(Hint: It's about 2.718...)

e ≈ 2.718281828... It's an irrational number that appears in continuous growth.

e ≈ 2.71828

More precisely: e = 2.718281828459045...

It is the base of natural logarithms and appears in continuous growth.

Answer: 2.71828

Problem 2 of 10 ⬜

Question: What is the derivative of $e^x$?

(Hint: e^x is its own derivative.)

The derivative of $e^x$ is itself: $\frac{d}{dx} e^x = e^x$.

$\frac{d}{dx} e^x = e^x$

This is the super-function property — e^x is its own derivative!

Answer: $e^x$

Problem 3 of 10 ⬜

Question: What is the integral of $e^x$?

(Hint: e^x is its own integral too.)

The integral of $e^x$ is itself: $\int e^x \, dx = e^x + C$.

$\int e^x \, dx = e^x + C$

e^x is its own derivative AND its own integral!

Answer: $e^x + C$

Problem 4 of 10 ⬜

Question: Find the derivative of $f(x) = e^{2x}$.

(Hint: The derivative of $e^{kx}$ is $k \times e^{kx}$.)

$k = 2$ in $e^{2x}$. So the derivative is $2 \times e^{2x} = 2e^{2x}$.

$f(x) = e^{2x}$

$f'(x) = 2 \times e^{2x} = 2e^{2x}$

Answer: $2e^{2x}$

Problem 5 of 10 ⬜

Question: Find the derivative of $f(x) = 3e^{x}$.

(Hint: The coefficient stays in front.)

$f(x) = 3e^x$. The 3 is a coefficient. The derivative of $e^x$ is $e^x$.

$f'(x) = 3 \times e^x = 3e^x$.

$f(x) = 3e^x$

$f'(x) = 3 \times e^x = 3e^x$

Answer: $3e^x$

Problem 6 of 10 ⬜

Question: Find the derivative of $f(x) = 4e^{3x}$.

(Hint: Multiply the coefficient by $k$.)

Coefficient = 4, $k = 3$.

$4 \times 3 = 12$. So $f'(x) = 12e^{3x}$.

$f(x) = 4e^{3x}$

$f'(x) = 4 \times 3 \times e^{3x} = 12e^{3x}$

Answer: $12e^{3x}$

Problem 7 of 10 ⬜

Question: Find the integral of $2e^{2x}$.

(Hint: Divide by $k$.)

$\int e^{kx} \, dx = \frac{1}{k} e^{kx} + C$.

For $2e^{2x}$, the derivative of $e^{2x}$ is $2e^{2x}$.

So the integral of $2e^{2x}$ is $e^{2x} + C$.

$\int 2e^{2x} \, dx = e^{2x} + C$

Check: The derivative of $e^{2x} + C$ is $2e^{2x}$. ✓

Answer: $e^{2x} + C$

Problem 8 of 10 ⬜

Question: What does the "Magic Bank Account" illustrate about $e^x$?

(Hint: Continuous growth at a rate equal to the current amount.)

In the Magic Bank Account, the interest added is always equal to the amount of money you have.

The Magic Bank Account illustrates continuous growth where the rate of growth is proportional to the current amount.

This is exactly what $e^x$ does — it grows at a rate equal to its current value.

Answer: It illustrates continuous growth where the rate is proportional to the current amount.

Problem 9 of 10 ⬜

Question: What is the derivative cycle for $e^x$?

(Hint: It's simpler than sine and cosine — just one step.)

The derivative of $e^x$ is $e^x$. The derivative of that is also $e^x$. And so on.

The derivative cycle for $e^x$ is:

$e^x \to e^x \to e^x \to e^x \to \cdots$

It never changes! Every derivative is the same function.

Answer: $e^x \to e^x \to e^x \to \cdots$ forever.

Problem 10 of 10 ⬜

Question: Why is $e^x$ called the "super-function"?

Think about what makes it special: it's the only function that equals its own derivative.

$e^x$ is called the super-function because it is its own derivative and its own integral.

  • $\frac{d}{dx} e^x = e^x$
  • $\int e^x \, dx = e^x + C$

No other function has this property!

Answer: e^x is called the super-function because it is its own derivative and its own integral.
