Chapter 10: The Circle Logic — Sines and Cosines

"Sine and cosine are the height and width of a point moving around a circle — the language of repetition."

Problem 1 of 10 ⬜

Question: What do sine and cosine measure on a unit circle?

(Hint: One measures height, the other measures width.)

Think about a point moving around a circle. One coordinate is the height (vertical), the other is the width (horizontal).

On a unit circle:

  • Sine measures the height (vertical position) of a point.
  • Cosine measures the width (horizontal position) of a point.

At angle $\theta$: the point is at $(\cos \theta, \sin \theta)$.

Answer: Sine measures height, cosine measures width.

Problem 2 of 10 ⬜

Question: What is the value of $\sin(0^\circ)$ and $\cos(0^\circ)$?

(Hint: At 0°, the point is at the rightmost edge of the circle.)

,

At 0°, the point is at (1, 0). So the width (cosine) is 1 and the height (sine) is 0.

At $0^\circ$, the point is at the rightmost edge of the circle: $(1, 0)$.

$\sin(0^\circ) = 0$

$\cos(0^\circ) = 1$

Problem 3 of 10 ⬜

Question: What is the value of $\sin(90^\circ)$ and $\cos(90^\circ)$?

(Hint: At 90°, the point is at the top of the circle.)

,

At 90°, the point is at (0, 1). So the width (cosine) is 0 and the height (sine) is 1.

At $90^\circ$, the point is at the top of the circle: $(0, 1)$.

$\sin(90^\circ) = 1$

$\cos(90^\circ) = 0$

Problem 4 of 10 ⬜

Question: What is the derivative of $\sin(x)$?

(Hint: The derivative of sine is cosine.)

The derivative of $\sin(x)$ is $\cos(x)$.

$\frac{d}{dx} \sin(x) = \cos(x)$

Answer: $\cos(x)$

Problem 5 of 10 ⬜

Question: What is the derivative of $\cos(x)$?

(Hint: The derivative of cosine is negative sine.)

The derivative of $\cos(x)$ is $-\sin(x)$.

$\frac{d}{dx} \cos(x) = -\sin(x)$

Answer: $-\sin(x)$

Problem 6 of 10 ⬜

Question: What is the derivative cycle for sine and cosine?

(Hint: $\sin \to \cos \to -\sin \to -\cos \to \sin$)

The derivative of $\sin$ is $\cos$. The derivative of $\cos$ is $-\sin$. The derivative of $-\sin$ is $-\cos$. The derivative of $-\cos$ is $\sin$.

The derivative cycle is:

$\sin(x) \to \cos(x) \to -\sin(x) \to -\cos(x) \to \sin(x)$

It takes four steps to return to the start.

Answer: $\sin \to \cos \to -\sin \to -\cos \to \sin$

Problem 7 of 10 ⬜

Question: Find the derivative of $f(x) = \sin(2x)$.

(Hint: Use the Chain Rule: derivative of $\sin(kx)$ is $k\cos(kx)$.)

For $\sin(kx)$, the derivative is $k\cos(kx)$. Here $k = 2$.

$f(x) = \sin(2x)$

$f'(x) = 2 \times \cos(2x) = 2\cos(2x)$

Answer: $2\cos(2x)$

Problem 8 of 10 ⬜

Question: Find the derivative of $f(x) = \cos(3x)$.

(Hint: derivative of $\cos(kx)$ is $-k\sin(kx)$.)

For $\cos(kx)$, the derivative is $-k\sin(kx)$. Here $k = 3$.

$f(x) = \cos(3x)$

$f'(x) = -3 \times \sin(3x) = -3\sin(3x)$

Answer: $-3\sin(3x)$

Problem 9 of 10 ⬜

Question: Find the integral of $\cos(x)$.

(Hint: The integral of cosine is sine.)

The integral of $\cos(x)$ is $\sin(x) + C$.

$\int \cos(x) \, dx = \sin(x) + C$

Check: The derivative of $\sin(x) + C$ is $\cos(x)$. ✓

Answer: $\sin(x) + C$

Problem 10 of 10 ⬜

Question: Why do sine and cosine repeat every $360^\circ$?

(Hint: Think about going around a circle.)

After one full rotation around a circle, you're back at the starting point. The height and width repeat.

Sine and cosine repeat every $360^\circ$ because they describe the height and width of a point moving around a circle.

After $360^\circ$ (one full circle), the point is back at the starting position.

So the height (sine) and width (cosine) are the same as at $0^\circ$.

Answer: Because they describe motion around a circle — after 360° (one full circle), you are back where you started.
