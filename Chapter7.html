Chapter 7: The Area Under the Curve

"The integral is the exact area under a curve — adding up infinitely many, infinitely thin rectangles."

Problem 1 of 10 ⬜

Question: If you draw a graph of speed over time, what does the area under the curve represent?

(Hint: Think about the odometer.)

Speed × Time = Distance. The area under the curve is the sum of speed × tiny time for every moment.

The area under a speed curve represents total distance.

Each tiny rectangle under the curve has area = speed × tiny time = tiny distance.

Adding up all the tiny rectangles gives the total distance.

Answer: Total distance

Problem 2 of 10 ⬜

Question: A car drives at a constant speed of 60 mph for 3 hours. What is the area under the speed curve?

(Hint: The area is a rectangle: base × height.)

Area of a rectangle = base × height. Base = 3 hours, height = 60 mph.

Area = base × height = 3 × 60 = 180

This is also the total distance: 180 miles.

Answer: 180

Problem 3 of 10 ⬜

Question: A car accelerates from 0 to 60 mph over 3 hours at a constant rate. What is the area under the speed curve?

(Hint: The area is a triangle: (base × height) ÷ 2.)

Area of a triangle = (base × height) ÷ 2. Base = 3 hours, height = 60 mph.

Area = (base × height) ÷ 2 = (3 × 60) ÷ 2 = 180 ÷ 2 = 90

This is also the total distance: 90 miles.

Answer: 90

Problem 4 of 10 ⬜

Question: Find the area under $y = 2x$ from $x = 0$ to $x = 5$.

(Hint: The area is a triangle. Base = 5, height = $2 \times 5 = 10$.)

Area of a triangle = (base × height) ÷ 2. Base = 5, height = 10.

$y = 2x$

At $x = 0$, $y = 0$. At $x = 5$, $y = 10$.

Area = (base × height) ÷ 2 = (5 × 10) ÷ 2 = 50 ÷ 2 = 25

Answer: 25

Problem 5 of 10 ⬜

Question: Find the area under $y = 2x + 3$ from $x = 0$ to $x = 4$.

(Hint: The area is a trapezoid: $(h_1 + h_2) \div 2 \times$ base.)

At $x = 0$, $y = 3$.
At $x = 4$, $y = 11$.
Base = 4.

Area of a trapezoid = $(h_1 + h_2) \div 2 \times$ base.

$h_1 = 3$, $h_2 = 11$, base = 4.

Area = $(h_1 + h_2) \div 2 \times$ base

Area = $(3 + 11) \div 2 \times 4 = 14 \div 2 \times 4 = 7 \times 4 = 28$

Answer: 28

Problem 7 of 10 ⬜

Question: Use the right-hand sum with 4 rectangles (width = 1) to estimate the area under $y = x^2$ from $x = 0$ to $x = 4$.

(Hint: Use the right edge of each rectangle for the height.)

IntervalRight EdgeHeight ($x^2$)WidthArea
0 to 1$x = 1$$1$1$1$

2 to 3$x = 3$$9$1$9$

Enter the total area (sum of the last column).

Add the areas: $1 + 4 + 9 + 16 = ?$

Total area = $1 + 4 + 9 + 16 = 30$

The exact area is $\frac{64}{3} \approx 21.33$.

The left-hand sum was 14 (underestimate). The right-hand sum is 30 (overestimate).

The average of the two is $(14 + 30) \div 2 = 22$, close to the exact value of 21.33.

Answer: 30

Problem 8 of 10 ⬜

Question: What happens to the rectangle estimate as you use more and thinner rectangles?

Think about fitting more rectangles under the curve. Do they cover the area better?

As you use more and thinner rectangles, the estimate gets closer to the exact area.

When the rectangles are infinitely thin, the estimate becomes exact — that's the integral.

Answer: The estimate gets closer to the exact area.

Problem 9 of 10 ⬜

Question: Complete this sentence: "The integral is the exact _____ under the curve."

(Hint: It's the space inside a shape.)

The integral adds up rectangles to find the space under the curve.

"The integral is the exact area under the curve."

This is one of the most important definitions in calculus!

Answer: area

Problem 10 of 10 ⬜

Question: If flow rate is 2 gallons per minute for 5 minutes, what is the area under the flow rate curve?

(Hint: Area = rate × time. The curve is a rectangle.)

Area = base × height = 5 minutes × 2 gallons/minute = ?

Area = base × height = 5 × 2 = 10

This is the total water: 10 gallons.

Answer: 10
