Chapter 6: The Odometer Trick

"Accumulation — Adding up tiny slices to find total distance."

Problem 1 of 10 ⬜

Question: What is the integral in calculus?

(Hint: Think about an odometer.)

The integral is like an odometer — it tells you the total amount accumulated over time.

The integral is the total accumulation — adding up all the tiny changes to get the big total.

Think of it like an odometer that adds up every tiny rotation of your wheels to give total distance.

It answers the question: "How much total change happened?"

Problem 2 of 10 ⬜

Question: A car drives at 50 mph for 2 hours and then 70 mph for 1 hour. What is the total distance?

(Hint: Distance = Speed × Time for each part, then add.)

First part: 50 mph × 2 hours = 100 miles.

Second part: 70 mph × 1 hour = 70 miles.

Add them together.

First part: 50 mph × 2 hours = 100 miles

Second part: 70 mph × 1 hour = 70 miles

Total distance = 100 + 70 = 170 miles

Answer: 170 miles

Problem 3 of 10 ⬜

Question: What does the area under a speed curve represent?

(Hint: Think about the odometer.)

Speed is the height of the curve. Time is the width. Area = height × width = speed × time = distance.

The area under a speed curve represents total distance.

Speed × Time = Distance.

When speed is changing, you add up all the tiny rectangles (speed × tiny time) to get the total area = total distance.

Answer: Total distance

Problem 5 of 10 ⬜

Question: What is the integral symbol and what does it mean?

(Hint: It looks like an elongated S.)

The symbol looks like an elongated 'S'. It stands for "sum" or "accumulate".

The integral symbol is $∫$.

It means "sum up" or "accumulate".

It comes from the letter S, which stands for "sum".

When you see $∫ f(x) dx$, it means: "Add up all the values of $f(x)$ times tiny pieces of $x$."

Problem 6 of 10 ⬜

Question: If you divide the area under a curve into 4 rectangles instead of 1, does the estimate get better or worse?

Think about more rectangles fitting the curve more closely.

Using more rectangles gives a better estimate.

The rectangles fit the curve more closely, leaving less empty space between the rectangles and the curve.

As the number of rectangles increases, the estimate gets closer to the exact area (the integral).

Answer: Better

Problem 8 of 10 ⬜

Question: In the integral notation $\int f(x) dx$, what does $dx$ represent?

(Hint: It's a tiny piece of something.)

Think about the width of a rectangle. $dx$ is a tiny, tiny width.

In $\int f(x) dx$, the $dx$ represents a tiny change in $x$ — a tiny width.

When you multiply the height ($f(x)$) by the tiny width ($dx$), you get the area of a tiny rectangle.

Then the integral $\int$ adds up all those tiny rectangles.

Answer: A tiny change in x (a tiny width)

Problem 9 of 10 ⬜

Question: A car accelerates smoothly from 0 to 60 mph over 3 hours. The speed curve is a straight line from (0,0) to (3,60). What is the total distance?

(Hint: The area under the line is a triangle.)

Area of a triangle = (base × height) ÷ 2.

Base = 3 hours, height = 60 mph.

The area under the speed curve is a triangle.

Base = 3 hours, height = 60 mph.

Area = (base × height) ÷ 2 = (3 × 60) ÷ 2 = 180 ÷ 2 = 90 miles

Check: Average speed = (0 + 60) ÷ 2 = 30 mph. Distance = 30 × 3 = 90 miles. ✓

Answer: 90 miles

Problem 10 of 10 ⬜

Question: Complete this sentence: "The derivative and the integral are ________."

(Hint: Think about addition and subtraction.)

Just like addition and subtraction undo each other, the derivative and integral undo each other.

"The derivative and the integral are opposites."

The derivative tells you how fast something is changing at a moment (speedometer).

The integral tells you the total accumulation (odometer).

They undo each other — this is the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus.

Answer: opposites
