Problem E1 of 12 ⬜

Question: What are the two big questions of calculus?

(Hint: Speedometer and odometer.)

Think about the speedometer (instant) and odometer (total).

The two big questions of calculus are:

  1. How fast is it changing NOW? (Derivative — speedometer)
  2. How much total change happened? (Integral — odometer)
Problem E2 of 12 ⬜

Question: Why is optimization important in data science?

(Hint: Think about training models and minimizing error.)

In machine learning, you find the minimum of an error function to make better predictions.

Optimization is important in data science because it is used to minimize error functions when training machine learning models.

When a model makes predictions, there is an error. By finding the minimum of the error function (using calculus), the model learns to make more accurate predictions.

This is called gradient descent — and it's powered by derivatives!

Problem E3 of 12 ⬜

Question: How does calculus help in physics?

(Hint: Think about motion, speed, acceleration.)

Speed is the derivative of position. Acceleration is the derivative of speed.

Calculus helps in physics by describing motion:

  • Position → Derivative = Speed
  • Speed → Derivative = Acceleration
  • Speed → Integral = Total Distance

Everything from falling objects to planetary orbits is described using calculus!

Problem E4 of 12 ⬜

Question: What role does $e^x$ play in finance and economics?

(Hint: Think about continuous compounding interest.)

When interest is compounded continuously, the growth follows e^x.

$e^x$ models continuous growth in finance and economics.

  • Compound interest: Money grows continuously with the formula $A(t) = P \cdot e^{rt}$
  • Population growth: Populations grow at rates proportional to their size
  • Market trends: Many economic models use exponential growth and decay

This is why $e^x$ is called the "universal growth function."

Problem E5 of 12 ⬜

Question: How are sine and cosine used in medicine?

(Hint: Think about heart rhythms and EKGs.)

The heart beats in a repeating cycle — sine waves model that rhythm.

Sine and cosine are used in medicine to model cycles and rhythms:

  • EKGs (heart monitoring): The heart's electrical activity is a repeating wave.
  • Brain waves: EEG signals are wave patterns.
  • Breathing cycles: The rhythm of breathing is periodic.
  • Drug concentrations: Some drugs create cyclic patterns in the body.

Any repeating biological process can be described using sine and cosine!

Problem E6 of 12 ⬜

Question: How does calculus help AI and machine learning learn?

(Hint: Think about gradient descent and optimization.)

AI learns by finding the minimum error. That's optimization — which uses derivatives.

AI and machine learning use calculus through gradient descent:

  • The model makes predictions and calculates error.
  • Using derivatives, the model finds the direction to adjust its parameters.
  • It keeps adjusting until the error is minimized (optimization).

This is exactly the optimization you learned in Chapter 11 — applied to millions of parameters!

The two questions of calculus and the Fundamental Theorem are the "code."

The final code of calculus tells you:
