Problem M1 of 15 ⬜

Question: What are the two questions that all of calculus answers?

(Hint: Think about speedometer and odometer.)

One question is about the speedometer (instant speed). The other is about the odometer (total distance).

The two questions of calculus are:

  1. How fast is it changing right now? (Derivative — like a speedometer)
  2. How much total change happened? (Integral — like an odometer)
Problem M2 of 15 ⬜

Question: In the function $y = 3x + 2$, what is $y$ when $x = 4$?

Multiply $x$ by 3, then add 2.

$y = 3x + 2$

When $x = 4$: $y = 3(4) + 2 = 12 + 2 = 14$

Problem M3 of 15 ⬜

Question: For $d = t^2$, what is the instant speed at $t = 4$ seconds?

(Hint: The derivative of $t^2$ is $2t$.)

Speed = $2 \times t$. Plug in $t = 4$.

Speed = $2 \times t = 2 \times 4 = 8 m/s$

Problem M4 of 15 ⬜

Question: Find the slope between the points $(1, 5)$ and $(4, 14)$.

(Hint: Slope = Rise ÷ Run.)

Rise = $14 - 5 = 9$, Run = $4 - 1 = 3$

Rise = $14 - 5 = 9$

Run = $4 - 1 = 3$

Slope = $9 \div 3 = 3$

Problem M5 of 15 ⬜

Question: Find the derivative of $f(x) = x^5$.

Bring the exponent down in front, then subtract 1.

$f(x) = x^5$

$f'(x) = 5x^{5-1} = 5x^4$

Problem M6 of 15 ⬜

Question: Find the derivative of $f(x) = 3x^4$.

Coefficient × exponent, then reduce exponent by 1.

$f(x) = 3x^4$

$f'(x) = 3 \times 4 \times x^{4-1} = 12x^3$

Problem M7 of 15 ⬜

Question: Find the antiderivative of $2x$.

What function has derivative $2x$? Don't forget the constant $C$.

$\int 2x \, dx = x^2 + C$

Check: derivative of $x^2 + C$ is $2x$. ✓

Problem M8 of 15 ⬜

Question: Find the area under $y = x^2$ from $x = 0$ to $x = 3$ using the Fundamental Theorem.

(Hint: Antiderivative of $x^2$ is $x^3/3$.)

$F(3) = 27/3 = 9$, $F(0) = 0$. Area = $F(3) - F(0)$.

$F(x) = \int x^2 \, dx = \frac{x^3}{3} + C$

$F(3) = \frac{27}{3} = 9$

$F(0) = 0$

Area = $9 - 0 = 9$

Problem M9 of 15 ⬜

Question: What is the derivative of $e^x$?

$e^x$ is its own derivative.

$\frac{d}{dx} e^x = e^x$

This is the super-function property!

Problem M10 of 15 ⬜

Question: Find the derivative of $f(x) = e^{3x}$.

The derivative of $e^{kx}$ is $k \times e^{kx}$. Here $k = 3$.

$f(x) = e^{3x}$

$f'(x) = 3 \times e^{3x} = 3e^{3x}$

Problem M11 of 15 ⬜

Question: What is the derivative of $\sin(x)$?

The derivative of sine is cosine.

$\frac{d}{dx} \sin(x) = \cos(x)$

Problem M12 of 15 ⬜

Question: Find the derivative of $f(x) = \cos(2x)$.

(Hint: derivative of $\cos(kx)$ is $-k\sin(kx)$.)

$k = 2$ in $\cos(2x)$. So derivative is $-2\sin(2x)$.

$f(x) = \cos(2x)$

$f'(x) = -2 \times \sin(2x) = -2\sin(2x)$

Problem M13 of 15 ⬜

Question: For $f(x) = -x^2 + 6x$, find the critical point.

(Hint: Find $f'(x)$ and set it to zero.)

$f'(x) = -2x + 6$. Set to zero: $-2x + 6 = 0$.

$f(x) = -x^2 + 6x$

$f'(x) = -2x + 6$

Set $f'(x) = 0$: $-2x + 6 = 0$

$2x = 6$

$x = 3$

Problem M14 of 15 ⬜

Question: A rectangle has a perimeter of 40 meters. What is the maximum possible area?

(Hint: The square maximizes area. Find the side length.)

$2x + 2y = 40$ → $y = 20 - x$.

$A(x) = x(20 - x) = 20x - x^2$.

Find the maximum at $x = 10$.

Perimeter: $2x + 2y = 40$ → $y = 20 - x$

Area: $A(x) = x(20 - x) = 20x - x^2$

$A'(x) = 20 - 2x$

Set $A'(x) = 0$: $20 - 2x = 0$ → $x = 10$

$y = 20 - 10 = 10$

Area = $10 \times 10 = 100$

Problem M15 of 15 ⬜

Question: Explain the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus in your own words. Why is it called the "Great Reversal"?

(Write a complete explanation.)

Before the theorem, slope and area were seen as separate. What did the theorem reveal?

The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus says that differentiation and integration are inverse operations — they undo each other.

  • If you differentiate and then integrate, you get back where you started.
  • If you integrate and then differentiate, you get back where you started.

It's called the "Great Reversal" because it reversed how people thought about calculus. Before the theorem, slope (derivative) and area (integral) were seen as separate. The theorem unified them into one elegant theory.

This is the single most important idea in calculus!
