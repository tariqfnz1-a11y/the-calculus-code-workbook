Chapter 1: The Big Secret

"Calculus is just arithmetic for things that move."

Problem 1 of 10 ⬜

Question: What are the two questions that all of calculus answers?

(Hint: Think about a car's speedometer and odometer.)

One question is about the speedometer (instant speed). The other is about the odometer (total distance).

The two questions of calculus are:

  1. How fast is it changing right now? (This is the derivative , like a speedometer.)
  2. How much total change happened? (This is the integral , like an odometer.)
Problem 2 of 10 ⬜

Question: What is the difference between average speed and instant speed ?

Think about a car trip. Average speed is the big picture (total distance ÷ total time). Instant speed is the specific moment (the speed at 2:15 PM exactly).

Average speed is total distance divided by total time. It's the big picture view of a journey.

Instant speed is the speed at a single, exact moment. It's the zoomed-in view.

Example: If you drive 60 miles in 1 hour, your average speed is 60 mph. But at exactly 2:15 PM, your instant speed might be 0 mph (if you're stopped at a red light) or 70 mph (if you're on the highway).

Problem 3 of 10 ⬜

Question: If a car travels 120 miles in 2 hours, what is its average speed ?

Average speed = Total distance ÷ Total time

Average speed = 120 miles ÷ 2 hours = 60 mph

This is the average speed. The actual speed at any given moment (instant speed) could be different.

Problem 4 of 10 ⬜

Question: Why is $0 \div 0$ a problem when calculating instant speed?

Speed = Distance ÷ Time. At a single moment, what is the distance traveled? What is the time taken?

At an exact instant, the distance traveled is 0 and the time taken is 0 .

So instant speed = $0 \div 0$, which is undefined — it doesn't make sense mathematically.

This is why calculus uses the limit trick (zooming in) instead of dividing directly.

Problem 5 of 10 ⬜

Question: What does the "Mental Zoom" help us visualize?

Think about zooming in on a curve until it looks like a straight line. What does the slope of that line tell you?

The Mental Zoom shows that when you zoom in far enough on any smooth curve, it looks like a straight line.

The slope of that straight line is the derivative (the instant speed).

This is the most important visual in calculus!

Problem 6 of 10 ⬜

Question: Complete this sentence: "Calculus is just arithmetic for ______."

Think about the title of Chapter 1. What is the "Big Secret"?

"Calculus is just arithmetic for things that move ."

This is the Big Secret of calculus!

Problem 7 of 10 ⬜

Question: If a car travels 60 mph for 3 hours, how far does it travel?

Distance = Speed × Time

Distance = 60 mph × 3 hours = 180 miles

Problem 8 of 10 ⬜

Question: The derivative is like a ________, and the integral is like an ________.

One tells you speed right now . The other tells you total distance .

The derivative is like a speedometer (tells you speed at an instant).

The integral is like an odometer (tells you total distance).

Problem 9 of 10 ⬜

Question: What is the "limit" trick used for in calculus?

When you can't calculate instant speed directly (because of $0 \div 0$), you look at smaller and smaller intervals and see what number the average speed is approaching .

The limit trick is used to find the instant speed by looking at smaller and smaller time intervals.

As the intervals get tiny, the average speed gets closer and closer to a specific number — that's the instant speed .

We never divide by zero. We just look at what the speed is approaching .

Problem 10 of 10 ⬜

Question: A car's distance is given by $d = 2t^2$. What is the instant speed at $t = 2$ seconds?

Hint: Use the pattern from Chapter 1: For $d = 2t^2$, speed = $4t$

Use the formula: Speed = $4t$. Plug in $t = 2$.

Given: $d = 2t^2$ and speed = $4t$

At $t = 2$ seconds:

Speed = $4 \times 2 = 8$ m/s

Answer: 8 m/s
